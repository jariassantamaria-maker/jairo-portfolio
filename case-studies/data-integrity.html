01 / THE CHALLENGE

The risk was
in the match.

Recurring reports brought together extracts from multiple sources. Inconsistent assignment codes could leave records unmatched or place information in the wrong reporting context.

My reporting work involved checking how records connected, investigating inconsistent assignments and preparing recurring and ad hoc analyses. I also documented cleaning steps, matching checks, exception handling and handover instructions.

At goFLUENT, I reviewed operational data daily for management and stakeholders and prepared recurring and ad hoc reports across daily-to-annual reporting cycles. The metrics covered call activity, retention, learner usage, training hours, email volumes, answered and unread WhatsApp messages, response times and escalations.

Semi-manual calculations could produce decimal discrepancies. I used Gemini and ChatGPT to assist parts of the processing and built reporting displays in Google Sheets using Google Apps Script and CSV extracts. The outputs were compared against Tableau, the original reporting source, as a validation step.

The contribution was a repeatable reporting workflow with source-based checks and documentation. No quantified reduction in errors or time savings is claimed.

Inspect the fictional rules & reference dictionary

What the rules allow

  1. Retain the raw value. Trim surrounding whitespace, remove an optional literal UNIT: prefix and convert the code to uppercase.
  2. Accept a code only when it consists of letters, a hyphen and two digits, such as BETA-02. Do not repair punctuation or guess a code.
  3. Use an exact match in the fictional dictionary. Unknown or ambiguous references require review.
  4. Compare repeated request IDs. An identical canonical payload keeps the first row and excludes later copies. Conflicting payloads hold every row with that ID for review.

Request IDs are compared after trimming surrounding whitespace and remain case-sensitive. No input is sent or saved.

04 / WHAT THIS DEMONSTRATES

The judgment
behind the report.

TRACEABILITY

Keep the original.

Raw and standardized values should remain distinguishable so someone can understand how a reporting decision was reached.

JUDGMENT

Hold the uncertain.

An unknown or ambiguous match is a question to investigate. A plausible assignment alone is not enough to treat it as validated.

CONTINUITY

Make it repeatable.

Clear rules, exception notes and handover instructions help another analyst follow and review recurring work.
