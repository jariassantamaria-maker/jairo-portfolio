CASE STUDY 12 / OPERATIONAL LEADERSHIP AND TRAINING

Keep operations running.
Teach the system.

Balancing daily service follow-ups with documentation, coaching, cross-team coordination and operational planning.

Operations specialist, trainer and peer mentor

Daily tracking routines, new-hire data onboarding, escalation instructions, work plans and operational training
01 Context02 Method03 Work performed04 Scope

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Make the work
repeatable.

Operational continuity required attention to case queues, class cancellations, international trackers, email follow-ups and exceptions while teams also needed a consistent way to learn new procedures.

I handled approximately 80 case follow-ups on a typical workday, while identifying recurring problems that warranted investigation rather than another ad hoc response.

02 / MY APPROACH

Investigate, clarify,
enable action.

  1. 01

    Prioritize the operational workload

    Review trackers and backlogs, identify urgent cases, and follow up with the owners of pending actions.

  2. 02

    Clarify ownership and escalations

    Map the appropriate teams for technical issues, permissions, enrollments, reporting, credits and learner communications.

  3. 03

    Teach and document

    Deliver data onboarding, explain incident-response rules and SOPs, and invite colleagues into improvement projects to gain different perspectives.

  4. 04

    Plan improvements alongside BAU

    Use team planning meetings and whiteboards to define monthly goals, reporting needs, responsibilities, deliverables and next steps.

03 / DELIVERABLES

What I actually
contributed.

Daily operational tracking included active learners, cancellations, consecutive absences, new Business Units, class incidents, email/WhatsApp activity and cross-regional follow-ups.

I trained new hires on data tools and processes and mentored an existing colleague who was being prepared for potentially broader responsibilities. I did not have a formal direct report.

I contributed to planning how dashboards and goal tracking could make performance clearer by region, Language Advisor and company, and documented cross-functional contact and escalation references.

04 / RESPONSIBILITY

Keep the claims
accurate.

The approximately 80 daily items describes my self-reported case follow-up workload, not 80 unique incidents resolved each day.

I trained and mentored colleagues, but did not hold a formal people-manager role or claim that all planned metrics or projects were implemented.
