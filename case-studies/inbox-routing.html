01 / THE CHALLENGE

An inbox is also
a decision queue.

Manual triage required classifying a request, matching it to a record and identifying the team responsible for the next step.

I analyzed the routing requirements and proposed rules based on issue type, request status and stakeholder ownership. The deliverables were a routing matrix, data requirements and an automation concept with human oversight.

The work remained a proposal. It did not establish a production Gmail automation or measured time savings.

02 / THE DESIGN APPROACH

Make the rule visible.
Keep judgment human.

Classify the work.
Explain the next step.

  1. 01

    Define the inputs

    Identify the request details and record information needed for a routing decision.

  2. 02

    Map ownership

    Relate the issue type and request status to a responsible team or existing owner.

  3. 03

    Expose exceptions

    Make uncertainty visible and leave decisions that need judgment with a person.

  4. 04

    Explain the handoff

    Keep the proposed destination and the information supporting it together.

THE MANUAL QUESTION

“Who should handle this?”

Classification, record matching and ownership must be considered together before choosing a destination.

Inspect the fictional rule order

The first matching rule wins. Later rules are not evaluated once a decision is reached.

  1. Protect sensitive decisions. A sensitive decision always goes to human review.
  2. Resolve conflicting matches. Conflicting records require a person to resolve ownership.
  3. Check the information. A missing record or missing required information pauses routing.
  4. Review closed requests. A closed request is reviewed before any reopening decision.
  5. Clarify the request. An unclear or mixed request needs classification review.
  6. Keep existing ownership. A complete, clear, open request returns to its existing demo owner.
  7. Route a new request. A complete, clear, new request follows its issue type.

REQUIREMENTS ANALYSIS

Define what is needed.

Turn a broad automation idea into specific inputs, conditions and ownership questions.

OPERATIONAL JUDGMENT

Make uncertainty actionable.

Distinguish a clear destination from a case that needs more information or a person’s decision.

REVIEWABLE LOGIC

Show the reason.

Explain the rule behind a proposed route so the decision can be understood and challenged.

Scope & outcome

The original deliverables were a routing matrix, data requirements and a proposal with human oversight. This new prototype illustrates those ideas with fictional rules. Production deployment, adoption and quantified business results are not claimed.
