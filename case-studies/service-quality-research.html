01 / THE CHALLENGE

Make the work
repeatable.

Reports such as “many classes failed” could reflect a genuine trend, an isolated incident or inconsistent record-keeping. Relying only on perceptions risked misclassifying user behavior or missing a recurring technical problem.

Learners reported friction across booking, audio/video, platform switches, lesson activities and course progress. These issues called for both direct user feedback and independent evidence checks.

02 / MY APPROACH

Investigate, clarify,
enable action.

  1. 01

    Listen independently

    Interview learners and gather first-hand qualitative feedback on their experience, even when no formal complaint was already assigned.

  2. 02

    Triangulate the records

    Compare learner accounts with session timestamps, network/audio indications, platform activity and case tracker notes.

  3. 03

    Investigate scope and cause

    Distinguish possible technical failures, process gaps and issues requiring third-party or Operations Support review. Avoid presenting a hypothesis as a confirmed cause.

  4. 04

    Escalate and follow through

    Document who needs to make the decision, track responses, confirm resulting classifications and coordinate communications back to the learner.

03 / DELIVERABLES

What I actually
contributed.

Investigated lessons where participants switched from a virtual classroom to Microsoft Teams soon after starting, comparing available timing, session behavior and reported technical issues to look for patterns.

Reviewed disputes about attendance and credit classifications, including when trainers and learners had conflicting accounts. I compiled evidence for accountable reviewers rather than claiming unilateral credit-approval authority.

Built a repeatable evidence-oriented approach: an incident is not “frequent” simply because it feels frequent; it should be counted, contextualized and compared against records over time.

04 / RESPONSIBILITY

Keep the claims
accurate.

Examples were anonymized and aggregated for this public case study. No real learner identities, employee emails, UUIDs or case identifiers are shown.

The work supports investigation and decision-making; not every suspected root cause was technically proven or every proposed process correction independently confirmed as implemented.
