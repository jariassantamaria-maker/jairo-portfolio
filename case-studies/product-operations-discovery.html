01 / WHAT I NOTICED

Workarounds are
product signals.

Operational teams and learners faced avoidable friction when information was scattered, navigation was unclear or tasks required manual reconciliation. A confusing interface could also create downstream misunderstandings about engagement or attendance.

I wrote down specific problems and possible requirements instead of leaving them as informal complaints. I wanted future changes to be testable, understandable and useful to the people doing the work.

02 / HOW I FRAMED IT

Observe. Specify.
Route for review.

  1. 01

    Identify the pain point

    Record friction in lesson discovery, booking, credit visibility, learning activities, user communication and administrative mapping.

  2. 02

    Make the request concrete

    Translate observations into specific behaviors: searchable lesson IDs, clearer filters, credit dates and balances, visible course progress or informative status fields.

  3. 03

    Consider the workflow

    Define ownership, input and output fields, escalation routes, required confirmations and possible automation or template behavior.

  4. 04

    Document before development

    Use SOP and proposal structure to clarify purpose, method, sources, stakeholders, timing, communication channels and limits of responsibility.

03 / SELECTED REQUIREMENTS

Ideas translated into
reviewable actions.

Navigation & reporting. Suggested easier access to lessons by identifiers and business unit, date-based views for scheduled classes, stronger organizational mapping and visual dashboards for learning consumption.

Credit & engagement clarity. Proposed clearer credit-line start and end dates, remaining balances, completed activities and attempts, so teams could interpret usage without guesswork.

Communications & automation. Considered more specific inquiry categories, response templates, standardized acknowledgments, inbox routing, automated reminders and clearer rules for which cases require human review.

Governance & consistency. Kept notes on ownership, request scopes, cross-team handoffs, status visibility and a structured SOP template with purpose, method, links, contacts, schedules and escalation boundaries.

04 / WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLAIM

Proposals are
not releases.

These notebook pages evidence identified opportunities, preliminary requirements and recommended next steps. They do not by themselves prove that the suggested Admin Center features were built, approved or launched.

Separate case studies document implementations that I did complete, such as standardized change-request workflows and Learner Widget Pro. This case demonstrates the discovery and business analysis work preceding potential product changes.
