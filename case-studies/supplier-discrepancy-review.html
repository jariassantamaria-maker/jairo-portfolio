01 / THE CHALLENGE

See the pattern
across orders.

Small shipping-cost differences on individual orders could accumulate into larger reconciliation gaps across a supplier portfolio. Unresolved items remained flagged and became backlog until corrected.

Supporting documents, including POD/POP, often arrived as scans with handwritten signatures. The review and compilation of recurring issues were manual.

As the point of contact between the supplier portfolio and the client, I needed to make pending discrepancies visible and connect them with the teams responsible for review.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

Turn repeated issues
into focused follow-up.

  1. 01

    Flag the difference

    Identify the shipping-cost discrepancy at order level and keep the pending item visible.

  2. 02

    Review the evidence

    Review the available scanned POD/POP and supporting records as part of the discrepancy review.

  3. 03

    Compile recurring patterns

    Manually compile reports of repeated issues by freight supplier, merchandise supplier and relevant contact.

  4. 04

    Coordinate the next action

    Use the patterns to focus reminders and escalate to Shipping or Billing, with follow-up routed to the responsible teams.

03 / STAKEHOLDER COMMUNICATION

Earlier reviews.
Shared context.

I participated in several calls each week with the client-facing team and directly with the client. The recurring-issue reports gave those conversations a clearer basis for reviewing pending discrepancies.

The reports helped focus attention on recurring patterns and supported reminders before month-end billing. Stakeholders could review an issue in the context of known small reconciliation differences.

Earlier visibility and clearer explanations helped build confidence in how discrepancies were being reviewed and escalated.

04 / QUALITATIVE OUTCOME

Make pending work
easier to discuss.

PRIORITIZATION

Focus the review.

Recurring patterns helped identify where additional attention and reminders were useful.

VISIBILITY

Review earlier.

Pending items had context for stakeholder discussions before the month-end billing process.

CONFIDENCE

Explain the discrepancy.

Shared evidence and clearer follow-up helped build stakeholder confidence in the handling of exceptions.

Scope & responsibility

My role was to identify, document, communicate and escalate discrepancies. Shipping and Billing retained responsibility for corrections. The outcome described here is qualitative; no measured reduction in backlog or processing time is claimed.
